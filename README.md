
## Use Cases for eu-ehds:HealthDataRequest

## Use Case 1
Description: This use case demonstrates the formal representation of a data access permit application for the secondary use of electronic health data under the European Health Data Space (EHDS) framework. Following the strict requirements of Article 53(1)(e), the scenario models a request where a "Health Data User" seeks access to sensitive datasets to conduct scientific research that contributes to public health and the development of oncology treatments. The core entity is the ehds:HealthDataRequest, which serves as the administrative anchor for the transaction. The request is initiated by a dpv:AcademicScientificOrganisation (the Recipient), targeting specific, high-granularity data categories: pd:HealthRecord for clinical history and eu-ehds:GenomicData for mutation markers. By using the specific eu-ehds term (a sub-concept of pd:Genetic as verified in the DPV 2.3 hierarchy), the model reflects the technical precision and data minimization principles required for EHDS compliance. To satisfy the legal requirement for "Purpose Specification," the request is linked to sector-health:HealthcareScientificResearch, a specialized purpose from the DPV Health extension. The administrative lifecycle is captured by the status dpv:RequestAcknowledged, signifying that the Health Data Access Body (HDAB) has formally received the submission and it is now entering the regulatory review phase. This model provides a machine-readable audit trail connecting the legal mandate (Art. 53), the organizational actor, and the specific data processing intentions.

## Use Case 2
Description: This example illustrates the representation of prohibited purposes for secondary health data use as defined under the European Health Data Space (EHDS). According to Article 54(d), access to health data must be refused when the intended processing involves the development of products or services detrimental to public health, such as illicit drugs, alcoholic beverages, tobacco and nicotine products, weaponry, or any services designed to create addiction. This use case shows how a Health Data Access Body (HDAB) can systematically evaluate and reject requests that contravene these essential public interest safeguards through a policy-based architecture.
This example shows a model structured in two functional layers: (1) a Policy Layer that defines the legal constraints; (2) Request Layer capturing the specific instance of a non-compliant access attempt. The first layer establishes a consolidated purpose, ex:HarmfulAndAddictiveProductDevelopment and provides the human-readable transparency required for regulatory auditing. This purpose is integrated into a dpv:Policy containing a dpv:Prohibition rule, acting as a machine-readable filter that identifies certain research and development activities as incompatible with the EHDS. The second layer demonstrates the practical application of this policy when a commercial actor, modeled as a dpv:ForProfitOrganisation, submits an ehds:HealthDataRequest for ehds:HealthcareProvisionAggregatedData. By matching the request’s intent against the prohibited policy, the model generates a formal rejection status of dpv:RequestDenied. This example also shows how the technical justification for such a refusal can be expressed in a machine-readable form using the term eu-gdpr:PurposeIncompatible, facilitating automated compliance flagging and consistent enforcement across the HealthData@EU infrastructure.

## Use Case 3
Description: This example describes the technical execution of a diagnostic development project where a private entity utilizes Medical Imaging and Personal Electronic Health Data to improve clinical tools. Under EHDS Article 53, the training, testing, and evaluating of health-related algorithms is a recognized permitted purpose for secondary data use, provided the activity contributes to the improvement of healthcare delivery or diagnostic accuracy. This scenario demonstrates the operational processing of high-dimensional clinical data, such as radiological images, while ensuring compliance with the stringent data protection requirements of the European Health Data Space.  The representation focuses on the active processing state, using the term dpv:ActivityOngoing to indicate that the AI development system is currently engaged with the data. It identifies the Electronic Health Record System as the primary implementation technology from which the clinical data originates, linking the digital research activity back to its healthcare source.The use case justifies the processing through two specific objectives: sector-health:TrainTestAndEvaluateHealthRelatedAlgorithms and sector health:DiagnosticOptimisation. These are paired with the core processing operations of dpv:Analyse and dpv:Train, which are required to refine the diagnostic accuracy of the AI model using Medical Imaging. To fulfill the strict security mandates of the EHDS for secondary use, the code incorporates sector-health:SecureProcessingEnvironment as a mandatory technical measure. This ensures that the dpv:PublicInterest legal basis is upheld by keeping the data within a protected, audited environment managed by the designated corporate entity.

## Use Case 4
Description: This example illustrates a data access request for secondary health data use within the European Health Data Space (EHDS), focusing on health data users permitted to access electronic health data for purposes that significantly contribute to public health, such as monitoring the long-term safety and clinical efficacy of medicinal products. This specific scenario demonstrates how a pharmaceutical entity can formally request access to aggregated datasets through the Health Data Access Body (HDAB) while being strictly governed by a machine-readable architecture of technical and legal constraints. The model is structured across two functional layers that separate administrative metadata from substantive safety requirements. The model establishes the lifecycle state of the application using the verified term dpv:RequestInitiated and identifies the applicant as an eu-ehds:HealthDataUser, ensuring the request is processed under the correct legal framework by explicitly defining the regulatory context as sector-health:SecondaryUse. To satisfy the non-negotiable safeguards required for the issuance of a data permit, the model incorporates the mandatory implementation of a sector-health:SecureProcessingEnvironment as a technical measure to prevent data leakage. The request is justified under the legal basis of dpv:PublicInterest, which permits the specific processing operations of analysis and aggregation for health-related research. To mirror the strict privacy protections of EHDS Article 54, the representation integrates a specific dpv:Prohibition rule that forbids the processing operation of dpv:Reidentify. By expressing these complex administrative and safety requirements in a standardized form, the example facilitates automated compliance auditing and consistent policy enforcement across the HealthData@EU infrastructure.

## Use Case 5
Description: In this example, a Health Data Request for a Pharmaceutical Budget Impact study is initiated to support evidence-based Policymaking. The request identifies the primary subject matter as Electronic Prescriptions, a specific category of health data defined within the EHDS framework. To operate within the permitted scope of Secondary Use in the health sector, the data must undergo a mandatory transformation. This is represented by the Anonymise processing activity, which serves as the technical and legal bridge between sensitive personal records and shareable insights. The execution of this process results in an Outcome known as a prescription report. While the input originated as personal health information, the resulting artifact is classified as Non-Personal Data since it has achieved a status of Anonymised Data. This transition ensures that the final output, an aggregated statistical report of medication counts, fulfils the strict "anonymised statistical format" requirements mandated by EHDS Article 69(2)(d). By linking the request directly to this anonymised outcome, the model provides a transparent account of how sensitive health data is ethically repurposed for the public interest without compromising individual privacy.
